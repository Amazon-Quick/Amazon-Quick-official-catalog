# Best practices

Treat the score as coaching and act on it. Each practice below names what to write and where to write it.

## Do

1. **Describe the customer's problem and quantify it.** State what is broken or slow today, what it costs the customer (spend, waste, lost time, risk), the scale (users, accounts, data, transactions), and the result they want. Use short labels such as Why now, Incumbent, Competition, Evaluation criteria, Contracting, and Budget authority so each element is easy to find.
2. **Say why the customer has to buy now, and by when.** A contract that expires, a compliance deadline, a failed audit, a price increase from their current vendor, or a mandate from their leadership. A go-live date alone does not explain why now.
3. **Name the buyer, the champion, and the proof the budget exists.** Give the approver's name and title, the person inside the customer championing the project, and the evidence: an approved budget line, a signed statement of work, or an existing AWS commitment the opportunity draws down. Add the champion (and the approver when known) as customer contacts too.
4. **Name the alternative, even when it is "do nothing."** State the incumbent, competing vendor, in-house option, or status quo, and the criteria and KPIs the customer will use to choose, with current baselines and targets where known.
5. **State the contracting path.** Say whether the opportunity transacts through AWS Marketplace (private offer or CPPO), directly, or through a reseller; whether an RFP or tender is expected; and when procurement starts and ends.
6. **Date the milestones.** Give calendar dates for discovery, evaluation, decision, and signature, in the description and again in next steps.
7. **Write next steps as one or two dated actions, at least one of them a customer meeting.** A discovery call, workshop, architecture review, or decision meeting with a named customer contact shows customer engagement; partner deliverables alone (send a plan, prepare a proposal) do not. Each action names your owner and a customer contact with title. Refresh after every customer meeting.
8. **Make a specific ask of AWS.** In the Partner Primary Need from AWS field, select the specific action you need, such as Technical Consultation, Architectural Validation, Pricing Assistance, or Business Presentation. Deal Support on its own counts as general support and does not count.
9. **Attach your solution and list the AWS services the customer will adopt.** List them in the AWS Products field and in the description, with the projected AWS monthly revenue and what drives it.
10. **Ask the agents at any time.** Chat with the agents in AWS Partner Central or through the MCP server from your CRM or Slack, make the edits they recommend, and watch the score recalculate. Keep the opportunity owner current so the guidance reaches the person who can act on it.

## Do not

1. **Do not reuse a boilerplate business problem across customers.** A description that fits any customer gives the agents no signal about this one and scores low; a stub such as "[Partner to complete]" scores lowest. If your use case is similar across customers, keep the constant parts and vary the customer's situation, business impact and timing, opportunity size, and stage of evaluation.
2. **Do not describe your product.** What your product does, or how your sales cycle is going, earns no credit. The description is about the customer, including why they cannot wait: a target close date tells AWS when, not why.
3. **Do not share confidential or proprietary information.** Leave out anything confidential between you and the customer, such as information under NDA the customer has not agreed to share with AWS, and your own proprietary information, such as margins, seller compensation, or how the opportunity benefits an individual AWS seller or partner seller. Customer details and contacts, pricing, and discounts are welcome. AWS rewards specifics about the customer's problem and decision, not confidential detail.
4. **Do not let your CRM or integration tool submit unchecked.** Review what it writes into the Customer Business Problem and Next Steps fields before it submits; a static product description repeated across opportunities is the most common cause of a low score.
5. **Do not name only your own people or only your own deliverables in next steps.** A next step with no customer-side contact, or with no customer meeting, reads as partner activity, not customer engagement.
6. **Do not rely on auto-generated or identical next steps.** Default text, or the same sentence repeated across opportunities, scores low.
7. **Do not list the AWS services your own product runs on.** List the services the customer is adopting.
8. **Do not split one engagement into several opportunities.** Near-identical records for the same customer are scored one by one but read as one opportunity by the AWS seller, and they crowd out the record that matters.
9. **Do not ask for AWS seller engagement unless a specific seller action changes the outcome.** Opportunities that do not need one progress with full agent support.
10. **Do not shorten or reword scored fields without adding detail.** The score is recalculated on every edit. Trimming a description or simplifying a next step can lower the score because the details the score credited are gone.
