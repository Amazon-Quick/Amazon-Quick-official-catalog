# How scoring works

Every opportunity is scored on submission, assigned a Co-sell motion, and rescored each time the AWS Partner edits a scored field. The Co-sell motion is assigned based on the Opportunity Quality Score and the strategic importance of the opportunity, which is determined based on the partner, the associated AWS customer, and the products and services involved. The AWS Partner controls only the Opportunity Quality Score, so raising it is the right action in every case.

## Co-sell motions

- **AWS Field-engaged.** An AWS sales team works the opportunity with the AWS Partner. Carries the opportunities with the highest Opportunity Quality Scores and strategic importance.
- **Agent-engaged.** AWS Partner Central agents qualify and enrich the opportunity with the AWS Partner. As the opportunity strengthens, it can move to AWS Field-engaged.
- **Partner-led.** The AWS Partner drives the opportunity with agent support. Every update is accepted by AWS immediately.

The Opportunity Quality Score and the final Co-sell motion become visible after AWS approves the submission. Right after submission the review status is Submitted and the score is not yet available.

## Score components

Four components make up the Opportunity Quality Score. Customer Business Problem Completeness is the primary driver.

| Component | Element | What a high-scoring submission includes |
|-----------|---------|------------------------------------------|
| Customer Business Problem Completeness | Customer's business problem | This customer's specific situation, what is forcing them to act now, and the outcome they expect |
| Customer Business Problem Completeness | Purchase and evaluation process | What the customer requires from a solution, how they will evaluate options, how and when they will decide, and the path to approval and signature |
| Customer Business Problem Completeness | Quantified outcomes and KPIs | The measures the customer will use to size the problem and judge a solution, with current baselines and targets |
| Customer Business Problem Completeness | Named contacts at the customer | Who can approve funding and who champions the project internally |
| Customer Business Problem Completeness | Competitive landscape | Other vendors, in-house options, or the status quo competing for the same budget |
| Next Steps | Specific action | A concrete action with a deliverable |
| Next Steps | Timeline | A specific future date tied to each action |
| Next Steps | Ownership | A named person responsible for each action |
| Next Steps | Customer contact | A named person at the customer involved in the next step |
| Next Steps | Customer meeting | At least one action is a meeting with the customer, not only a partner deliverable |
| Partner Primary Need from AWS | Specific need | A precise ask naming what the AWS seller should do, such as Technical Consultation, Architectural Validation, Pricing Assistance, or Business Presentation |
| Solution and AWS Products | Partner solution | The AWS Partner's registered solution attached to the opportunity |
| Solution and AWS Products | AWS products | The AWS services the customer is adopting, listed in the description and the AWS Products field |
