# Writing guide

All names, companies, and figures here are fictitious examples, not real financial results.

## Line comments

- 20 words or fewer. Lead with the driver, not the symptom.
- Include the number that explains the move (the rate, count, or amount).
- Use only Data-supported or Owner-stated causes. Mark a line with Unexplained pieces "[partly unexplained]", or "[unexplained]" when nothing explains it.
- Use F and U consistently, and "partly offset by" for netting.

| Line | Variance | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractor fees | +$0.7M (U) | Implementation contract ran six weeks past plan at about $117K a week (PMO lead, 2026-11-03). | Contractor costs were higher than expected. |
| Revenue | +$2.7M (F) | Seat volume +6% (+$2.0M), Enterprise price increase (+$0.5M; pricing lead, 2026-11-04), and mix toward Pro (+$0.2M). | Revenue beat plan due to strong performance. |
| Software licenses | −$0.3M (F) | License count 40 below plan; renewal invoice not yet received to confirm. [partly unexplained] | Savings from license optimization. |
| Gross margin % | +6 bps (F), not material; shown as an example | Revenue was 4.9% over budget and cost of goods sold (COGS) 4.8% over; see the revenue and COGS lines. | Margin improved 0.2%. |

The bad examples fail because they restate the symptom, give no number, or assert a cause nobody confirmed.

## Headline, outlook, open items, and asks

Headline (fictitious example): "October operating income was $15.3M, $0.9M (6.3%) favorable to budget. Revenue was $2.7M over budget on seat volume (+$2.0M), the Enterprise price increase (+$0.5M, per the pricing lead), and mix toward Pro (+$0.2M), partly offset by $1.1M of higher cost of goods sold and $0.7M of higher operating expenses."

Outlook: "The PMO lead says the implementation contract ends November 14 (one-time). The pricing lead says the Enterprise price increase recurs."

Open items:

| Item | Amount | Needed | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Travel over budget | +$0.3M (U) | October expense reports by department | Finance ops | November 12 |
| Licenses under budget: saving or delayed renewal? | −$0.3M (F) | Renewal invoice or vendor confirmation | IT procurement lead | November 12 |

Asks: "Decision for the CFO by November 13: accept travel as unexplained for October, or hold the commentary until expense reports are in. From IT procurement by November 12: confirm whether the license renewal moved to November."

What makes these work: the headline answers "on plan or not, by how much, why" in two sentences; the outlook says what recurs; every gap has an owner and a date; the asks say who decides what, by when.

## Tone and format

- Factual and specific. No "significant", "strong performance", "headwinds", or "business conditions" without a number and a cause.
- One decimal for %, pp for ratios, and money in the table's unit ($M to one decimal).
- Active voice. Name teams, vendors, or programs only when the user or an owner did.
- Executive audience: headline, line comments, outlook, open items, and asks; checks and the ledger go in the appendix. Working-level audience: the same, with the ledger table moved up under the line comments.
- Keep method out of the commentary: no sign flips, unit conversions, rounding, or tool names above the appendix.
